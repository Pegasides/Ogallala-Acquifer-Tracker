Ogallala Aquifer Hydrogeological Status Report

A visual assessment of groundwater depletion, saturation thickness anomalies, and structural declines across the High Plains Basin.

1. Regional Decline and Storage Extremes

Decades of intensive agricultural extraction have introduced severe hydrological changes. The official map below highlights significant drawdown hotspots concentrated heavily across the southern half of the basin.

Figure 1: Cumulative water-level changes from predevelopment to recent monitoring intervals. Dark orange/red vectors map net declines exceeding 150 feet.
  • Southern High Plains (Texas & New Mexico): Massive, contiguous depletion corridors with localized water table collapses.
  • Central High Plains (Kansas & Oklahoma): High extraction zones matching industrial irrigation networks, causing severe localized cones of depression.
  • Northern High Plains (Nebraska): Retains a larger percentage of saturated thickness, though developmental pressures continue to scale up.

2. Center-Pivot Extraction Footprint

The transition to center-pivot sprinkler distribution systems catalyzed massive crop yields but severely altered the basin's natural water equilibrium. Natural recharge rates from sparse regional precipitation fail to offset current industrial extraction velocities.

Figure 2: Infrastructure profile of center-pivot distribution rigs pulling directly from localized deep-well groundwater columns.

Once storage resources drop past operational thresholds, localized pumping structures require deeper drilling intervals, leading to escalated infrastructure expenses and reduced operational lifespans for regional agriculture.
